## Generating point groups

Column 6 of the print, "Generating point groups with oriented face (site) symmetries between
parentheses": one row per point group printed in an entry, in printed order, with its face
symmetries as printed and the type it is printed in: `basic` for bold ("Basic (general and
special) forms are printed in bold face"), `limiting` for normal type ("limiting (general and
special) forms in normal type"). Braces, which connect the settings of 32, 3m, -3m, -42m and -6m2,
are dissolved into one row per setting; the third line of a brace for 32, 3m and -3m is the
description with rhombohedral axes (as in Table 3.2.3.2) and is marked `rhombohedral` in the column
Axes. Where the print joins two face symmetries of one group with "and" (footnote ‡: once bold,
once normal), they are two rows. A dagger `†` (footnote: the limiting form occurs in two or three
non-equivalent orientations) is kept in the column Note.

Footnotes as printed (p. 726): "† These limiting forms occur in three or two non-equivalent
orientations (different types of limiting forms); cf. Table 3.2.3.2. ‡ In point groups 4̄2m and
3̄m, the tetragonal prism and the hexagonal prism occur twice, as a 'basic special form' and as a
'limiting special form'. In these cases, the point groups are listed twice, as '4̄2m(.2.) and
4̄2m(..m)' and as '3̄m1(.2.) and 3̄m1(.m.)'."

Face symmetries are printed oriented (`..m`) where the orientation matters and otherwise without
dots (`3`, `4mm`, `2, m` for 2/m), as transcribed.

Errata. Two entries disagree with Table 3.2.3.2 and with the other entries of this table; they are
transcribed as printed, and the tests apply the corrections:

- No. 14 prints `4/mmm(.m, .m.)`; the tetragonal dipyramids of 4/mmm are the positions f `.m.` and e
  `..m` (Table 3.2.3.2, p. 752), so `.m` is to be read `..m` (a dot missing).
- No. 31 prints `6/m(1)` in normal type; in 6/m the hexagonal dipyramid is the first and only form
  of the general position c (Table 3.2.3.2, p. 760), a basic form, so it is to be read bold, as
  `4/m(1)` is printed bold in No. 14.
